\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
The experimental design pairs a stratified $180$-prompt baseline with matched downstream causal tests across three model families, evaluated by a human-validated judge ($\kappa = 0.74$) and a calibrated leakage detector. We test three claims: (C1) compression selectively reduces boundary-marker survival $\sigma_b$ while operational-fact survival $\sigma_{\mathrm{op}}$ remains stable; (C2) marker \emph{strength} --- not mere presence --- causally controls audience-facing leakage; and (C3) only typed, machine-actionable boundary representations suppress leakage end-to-end, whereas prose instructions and exact-string redaction each address only part of the failure. The remainder of this section presents the research questions and experiment mapping (\S\ref{sec:rqs}), the models (\S\ref{sec:models}), prompt counts (\S\ref{sec:counts}), the leakage definition (\S\ref{sec:leakage-definition}), and the evaluator and reporting protocol (\S\ref{sec:results_eval}); reproduction-irrelevant implementation details are deferred to~\cref{app:dataset,app:run}.

\subsection{Research Questions and Experiments}
\label{sec:rqs}
 
The study is organized around three questions. \textbf{RQ1.} Does compression selectively reduce $\sigma_b$ while $\sigma_{\mathrm{op}}$ remains stable? \textbf{RQ2.} When sensitive content is forwarded, does \emph{marker strength} --- the explicitness with which a boundary is phrased, on a vague-hedge-to-imperative gradient (operationalized as five levels L0--L4 in Experiments E3, E4, and E8 below) --- causally determine whether forwarded content becomes audience-facing leakage? \textbf{RQ3.} Among three candidate enforcement strategies --- prompt-only instructions to preserve markers, deterministic exact-string redaction of sensitive phrases, and a typed audience-allowlist schema --- which actually prevent leakage while preserving task utility?

\subsection{Models}
\label{sec:models}
GPT-5-mini \citep{singh2025openai} and DeepSeek-R1-32B \citep{deepseekai2025deepseekr1incentivizingreasoningcapability} are the two \emph{primary models} on which every experiment is run; Qwen3-32B \citep{yang2025qwen3} is included as a \emph{targeted third-model replication}, covering the four experiments where cross-pipeline robustness is load-bearing for the headline claims: E1 baseline handoff survival, E2 compression stress, E8 marker-strength gradient, and the E9 corrupted-allowlist stress test. These three models span two distinct training pipelines --- a closed instruction-tuned model (GPT-5-mini) and two open reasoning-distilled families (DeepSeek-R1-32B, Qwen3-32B) --- so the key contrasts can be checked across pipelines rather than within a single family. All runs use temperature 0.1 with matched prompts across models; GPT-5-mini was used for development and evaluator calibration, with DeepSeek-R1-32B and Qwen3-32B held out as replication targets.

\subsection{Prompts and Counts}
\label{sec:counts}
All prompts are drawn from the \textsc{Bound-Handoff} testbed (\S\ref{sec:dataset}). E1 uses a stratified 180-prompt subsample of the 720-cell design balanced across the 36 scenarios, four handoff surfaces, and five handoff conditions; E2 adds two hard-budget conditions (40-word and 25-word) over all 36 scenarios, yielding 72 additional handoff prompts. Downstream leakage probes (E3--E5, E8--E9) use 12 matched scenarios drawn from the testbed, crossed with marker variants and four pressure conditions. Full prompt counts and manifests are in~\Cref{app:counts}.

% E1 uses a stratified 180-prompt subset balanced across the 36 scenarios, five handoff conditions, and four handoff surfaces. E2 adds two hard-budget conditions (40-word and 25-word) over all 36 scenarios. E3 yields $12 \times 3 \times 4 = 144$ downstream prompts per model; E4 yields $12 \times 4 \times 4 = 192$; E8 extends the marker-strength axis to five levels (L0--L4) for a $12 \times 5 \times 4 = 240$ design per model (overlapping with E3/E4 endpoints, so 96 new prompts per model); E5 yields $12 \times 3 \times 4 = 144$ direct prompts per model. E6 evaluates 72 generated handoffs $\times\,4$ downstream pressures $= 288$ downstream prompts per model. E7 reruns the same 288 downstream prompts on redacted handoffs. E9 supplies a hand-constructed typed audience-allowlist schema for the 12 E3 scenarios and runs downstream evaluation under the four pressures ($12 \times 4 = 48$ prompts per model). Per-condition prompt manifests and the stratification scheme appear in~\cref{app:run}.

\subsection{Leakage Definition}
\label{sec:leakage-definition}

We distinguish context dump from final leakage. A context dump occurs when sensitive content appears in an internal handoff artifact, trace, or context field, with or without its governing marker. Final leakage occurs only when the downstream agent reveals that sensitive content in the audience-facing output. This distinction is central to the downstream causal contrasts: context dump measures internal boundary failure, while final leakage measures externally visible disclosure.

\subsection{Evaluation and Reporting}
\label{sec:results_eval}
Final leakage is scored by a scenario-specific exact/alias detector with word-boundary matching against disallowed phrases and curated aliases. The detector's phrase and alias lists were constructed by manually labeling all 144 GPT-5-mini E3 downstream outputs, so on those rows it achieves precision, recall, and accuracy of $1.0$ by construction. We therefore treat the detector as \emph{calibrated} for matched E3-style settings rather than as a universal guarantee, and supplement it with targeted manual audits on E4 weak-marker and E7 redaction outputs, where exact matching is known to miss paraphrase or category-level leakage. Boundary-marker and operational-fact survival are scored by a GPT-5.5 Thinking judge using the four-level rubric in \Cref{sec:method}; on a stratified $100$-item human-labeled E1 sample the judge reaches accuracy $0.92$ and Cohen's $\kappa = 0.74$. We report means with bootstrap $95\%$ CIs over scenarios; detector rules, judge prompts, labeling protocol, and per-condition breakdowns are in \Cref{app:run,app:results}.
