\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems often coordinate by compressing an upstream interaction into a handoff artifact that downstream agents treat as shared state. We show that this handoff step is a structural source of privacy leakage not because summaries lose information uniformly, but because they preferentially preserve operational facts while weakening the boundary metadata that governs how those facts may be used. We call this failure mode \emph{summary collapse}.

Using a controlled multi-agent coordination testbed and a judge-based marker-survival measure $\sigma$ (human-validated at accuracy $0.92$, $\kappa = 0.74$), we find that boundary-marker survival and operational-fact survival are nearly uncorrelated at the handoff level on both \texttt{gpt-5-mini} and \texttt{deepseek-r1:32b} (Pearson $r = -0.042$ and $0.086$): operational content can survive intact while boundary metadata is silently stripped. Under 25-word compression---within the range of aggressive production-style memory-compression budgets---$\sigma$ falls to $0.690$/$0.580$ on GPT/DeepSeek while operational survival stays at $0.997$/$0.972$. Controlled downstream tests reveal that protection depends on \emph{boundary explicitness}: vague hedges like ``use discretion'' leak in $73\%$ of GPT cases and $50\%$ of DeepSeek cases, while explicit constraints reduce leakage to under $15\%$ on both models. Prompt-only mitigations and exact-string redaction each address only part of the failure --- paraphrase/category leakage persists --- while a gold-derived upper-bound probe --- in which boundaries are encoded as a typed audience-allowlist schema constructed from scenario annotations --- eliminates leakage ($0/48$ on both models), isolating the operationalization axis from the boundary-extraction problem.
\end{abstract}
