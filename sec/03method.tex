\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We study summary collapse through \textsc{Bound-Handoff}, a controlled testbed that isolates what happens to boundary metadata when an upstream multi-agent interaction is transformed into a handoff artifact for a downstream agent. We distinguish a \emph{context dump} (sensitive content appears in an internal handoff artifact or context field, with or without its governing marker) from \emph{final leakage} (the downstream agent reveals that content in its audience-facing output). Context dump is a routing failure inside the MAS; final leakage is the externally observable harm.

\subsection{Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

\textsc{Bound-Handoff} contains 36 synthetic coordination scenarios across seven domains: scheduling, enterprise IT, HR, customer support, legal/compliance, medical administration, and project management. Each scenario contains an upstream transcript, gold operational facts needed for the downstream task, gold boundary markers governing sensitive facts that are intentionally unnecessary for the downstream task, and a downstream task description. Marker categories cover \emph{audience constraints}, \emph{ownership claims}, \emph{hedges}, and \emph{disclosure caveats}, following Communication Privacy Management. We evaluate four handoff surfaces (\texttt{explicit\_summary}, \texttt{forwarding\_to\_summary}, \texttt{memory\_replay}, \texttt{report\_writer}) and five handoff formats varying compression pressure and marker support. Full scenario manifests, marker examples, and prompt templates are in~\Cref{app:dataset}.

\paragraph{Why a controlled synthetic testbed?}
Our goal is to isolate a handoff-level mechanism, not estimate deployment prevalence. The causal contrasts in E3--E9 require gold operational facts, gold boundary markers, sensitive facts known to be unnecessary for the downstream task, and matched counterfactual variants that differ only in boundary representation. To our knowledge, no existing public dataset combines multi-agent handoff structure with counterfactual audience-boundary annotations. We therefore interpret the results as controlled mechanism evidence rather than production prevalence estimates.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:measures}

For each gold marker or operational fact, an LLM judge assigns one of four labels: preserved (1.0), paraphrased (0.75), weakened (0.35), or absent (0.0). \emph{Boundary-marker survival} $\sigma$ is the average score over gold boundary markers; \emph{operational-fact survival} $\sigma_{\mathrm{op}}$ is the analogous average over operational facts. \emph{Compression ratio} is $\rho = 1 - |h|/|u|$, where $u$ is the upstream transcript and $h$ the handoff artifact (positive $\rho$ indicates compression; negative $\rho$ a longer artifact). The selective-fragility claim is that $\sigma$ falls under compression while $\sigma_{\mathrm{op}}$ remains near ceiling. The full judge prompt template is in~\Cref{app:judge}.
