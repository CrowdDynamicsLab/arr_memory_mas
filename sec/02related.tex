\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Privacy and contextual norms in LM agents.}
Prior benchmarks such as ConfAIde~\citep{mireshghallah2023can}, PrivacyLens~\citep{shao2024privacylens}, and AgentDAM~\citep{zharmagambetov2025agentdam} test whether language models respect context-dependent disclosure norms in single-agent decisions, building on contextual integrity~\citep{nissenbaum2009privacy}. We instead study the intermediate handoff artifact where the fact and its disclosure rule may separate.

\paragraph{Multi-agent privacy and communication channels.}
Work on agent memory~\citep{wang2025unveiling}, inter-agent communication~\citep{lee2024prompt, wang2025ip, he2025red}, compositional aggregation~\citep{patil2025sum}, and prompt or environmental attacks~\citep{alizadeh2025simple, liao2024eia} shows that multi-agent systems introduce new leakage surfaces. Our setting is non-adversarial: the sensitive fact is already present and often accompanied by an explicit boundary marker, but compression weakens the marker.

\paragraph{Summarization fidelity.}
Abstractive summarization can drop hedges, attributions, and conditions~\citep{maynez2020faithfulness}. We study the multi-agent privacy analogue: the dropped qualifier is the rule that licenses the surviving fact, and the downstream cost is final audience-facing leakage rather than factual error.

\paragraph{Communication Privacy Management.}
CPM~\citep{petronio1991communication, petronio2002boundaries, griffin2006first} distinguishes private information from the boundary rules governing co-ownership and disclosure. We operationalize this distinction in handoff summaries by measuring survival of boundary markers separately from operational facts, and identify the cross-phase summary as a structural source of boundary loss in MAS. Extended positioning and a fuller comparison to adjacent attack and memory-extraction literature are in~\Cref{app:extended_related}.
